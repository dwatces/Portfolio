Questions people ask before hiring a web designer.

What does it cost?

My small-business sites and redesigns usually cost $2,000–$8,000 on a fixed quote, online stores start from $6,000, and web applications are quoted by stage. After launch, a .co.nz domain is about $20–40 a year, hosting for a typical business site is free to about $30 a month, and any platform subscription is listed up front. My NZ website cost guide compares the wider market.

How long does it take?

A typical small-business site takes 2–4 weeks once your content is ready, and an online store 4–8 weeks. Web applications run in stages, with milestones agreed first.

What’s the difference between a web designer and a web developer?

A web designer decides how a site looks, reads and leads people to get in touch. A web developer builds it and makes the forms, bookings and payments work. Agencies often split the two; with me, it’s one job.

Do I own it, and can I edit it myself?

Your domain, hosting and analytics sit in accounts in your name, and at launch I hand over the agreed code. You can make the everyday edits yourself.

Can you fix or redesign my existing site?

Often, yes: not every site needs a rebuild, and a maintained WordPress site can stay on WordPress. A free review of your site will show whether it needs a few fixes or a full website redesign.
