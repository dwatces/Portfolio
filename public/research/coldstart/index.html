The equivariance law, held on real city data

Equivariant models pay exactly when the symmetry is present and data is scarce. New-station demand forecasting across five cities × two seasons: the hex-equivariant model beats the directional one in 10 of 10 city-seasons; the gain holds while few stations have history and shrinks once most do.

In cold-start forecasting a brand-new station has no history of its own, so the spatial operator is the whole model — exactly where a symmetry prior can pay.

It does, in 10 of 10 real point-level bike-share city-seasons: the mean gain stays roughly level while up to 40% of stations have history, and is much smaller once 80% do. Matched parameters, multiple seeds — equivariance pays if and only if the symmetry is genuinely present.
